Why a unified registry

Today, a landlord with units in both Oakland and Berkeley has to register on two different platforms with two different vendors, two different login systems, and two slightly different versions of the same questions. Same data, twice the work, and it never lands in the same place.

What stays the same

Each city keeps its own ordinance, fee schedule, deadlines, and exemption rules. This platform routes each landlord's submission to the right city's program and only asks the questions that city's ordinance actually requires.

What's new

For the first time, agency analysts can see a regional view of who's renting to whom, at what rents, with what turnover — without forty FOIA requests. Cell suppression and access controls keep individual tenants protected.

Your city runs a Rent Stabilization Ordinance (RSO). These questions certify the base rent on file, this year's allowable increase, and any pass-throughs or tenant buyouts from the past 12 months. The answers become part of the public rent history for each unit.

The base rent is the initial legal rent when the current tenancy began — not today's rent. It's the anchor your city uses to calculate every future allowable increase.

This year's allowable annual increase

Your city sets an annual cap tied to the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Increases above this cap require a separate petition.

2026 allowable increase: 2.3%
Based on 60% of the 12-month change in the Bay Area Consumer Price Index (CPI). Notice must be served at least 30 days before the effective date (60 days if the increase exceeds 10%).
Banking of unused increases

Some years you may not raise rent by the full cap. Cities that allow "banking" let you carry unused percentage points forward, subject to per-year and lifetime limits.

Capital improvement pass-throughs

If you have a city-approved capital improvement (roof, seismic, elevator, etc.) with an active pass-through to tenants, list the petition number and monthly amount per unit.

Tenant buyout / cash-for-keys agreements

Rent-stabilization cities require disclosure of any payment made to a tenant in exchange for voluntarily vacating. Attach the signed buyout agreement if one applies.

Median rent in East Palo Alto rose 5.7% year-over-year — the highest jump in the region.

The Bay Area median rent rose 3.1% in the past 12 months, but East Palo Alto stands out at +5.7%. Most of the increase came from 2-bedroom units in census tract 6113 (Belle Haven), where median rent moved from $2,890 to $3,050. This tract also saw the highest rate of owner-initiated move-outs (4.2% vs. regional avg 1.8%), suggesting turnover-driven repricing.
